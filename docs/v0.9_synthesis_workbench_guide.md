# Study Framework + Synthesis Workbench Guide (v0.9 Phase 6)

## 1. Overview & Purpose

Field Learning Studio v0.9 Phase 6 introduces the **Study Framework + Synthesis Workbench**—a comparative analytical workspace that reduces evaluator cognitive load while strengthening the path from validated field evidence to defensible findings.

In conventional evaluation and MEL workflows, moving from qualitative observations to findings is often slow, fragmented, and prone to confirmation bias. Practitioners are forced to manually juggle dozens of individual notes, remember disconnected interview quotes across sites, or fill out rigid four-part templates (*Finding → Lesson → Good Practice → Recommendation*) even when the data only supports an actionable recommendation.

Phase 6 replaces disconnected card review with an integrated, comparative analytical workspace. The progression follows a disciplined evidentiary spine:

$$\mathbf{\text{Study Questions}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Validated Evidence}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Comparative Workspace}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Working Pattern}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Draft Finding}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Support Profile}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Human Validation}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Downstream Outputs}}$$

---

## 2. Core Architectural Philosophy: The Analytical Spine

### 2.1 Study Questions as the Organizational Anchor
Rather than grouping findings post-hoc, the workspace establishes **Study Questions** (`StudyQuestion`, `RQ-***`) upfront as the analytical spine.
- Each study question is tagged with standard evaluation criteria (e.g. *Effectiveness*, *Relevance*, *Coherence*, *Efficiency*, *Impact*, *Sustainability*, or *Cross-Cutting*).
- Validated evidence entries (`EV-***`) can be tagged to one or more study questions via the Evidence Matrix or directly in the Synthesis Workbench.
- The UI provides an **Analytical Spine Filter Bar** where practitioners select a question to instantly view all relevant evidence across sites and stakeholder groups, or click **Unassigned Evidence** to triage evidence not yet mapped to research questions.

### 2.2 Rejecting the Mandatory Four-Sequence Pipeline
Phase 6 explicitly eliminates the cumbersome requirement that every finding must spawn a lesson, a good practice, and a recommendation.
- **Findings** represent defensible empirical claims backed by triangulated evidence.
- **Recommendations** represent actionable operational proposals anchored directly in validated findings.
- **Lessons Learned** and **Good Practices** are treated as **flexible, optional analytical outputs**. Evaluators toggle them on or off at the study level using `StudyOutputConfig`.

## 3. Comparative Workspace Architecture (`SynthesisComparisonView`)

To prevent evaluators from needing to open and remember individual evidence cards, the Synthesis Workbench provides dynamic multi-dimensional comparative lenses:

1. **Compare by Site**: Renders side-by-side columns for each field site (e.g., Minya Rural vs. Assiut Urban), enabling immediate identification of geographical variations, local bottlenecks, and commonalities.
2. **Compare by Stakeholder**: Contrasts perspectives across participant roles (e.g., Teachers vs. Parents vs. Directorate Officials) to expose perception gaps or conflicting priorities.
3. **Compare by Method**: Groups observations by data collection methodology (e.g., Key Informant Interviews vs. Focus Group Discussions vs. Direct Observations) to evaluate triangulation depth.
4. **Compare by Theme**: Groups evidence under primary thematic categories (e.g., *Nutrition Access*, *Supply Chain*).
5. **Matrix View**: Cross-tabulates evidence across two dimensions (e.g., Site $\times$ Method or Site $\times$ Theme) with visual coverage indicators and missing data alerts.

### Common Theme Detection
When grouping by Site, the workbench automatically analyzes evidence across columns and displays a **Common Themes Across Multiple Sites** indicator, highlighting themes with confirmed multi-site representation.

---

## 4. Working Patterns & Sensemaking Notes (`PatternNote`)

Between messy field observations and formal findings lies an essential sensemaking step: recognizing recurring patterns without premature commitment to high-stakes claims.

- **Working Patterns** (`PAT-***`) allow evaluators to record emergent cross-source patterns, thematic tags, and observed contradictions.
- Pattern notes explicitly reference supporting evidence items (`linkedEvidenceIds`) and can be linked to an overarching Study Question (`studyQuestionId`).
- **One-Click Finding Promotion**: Evaluators can click `Promote to Draft Finding` on any pattern note to pre-populate the Finding Authoring Modal with the pattern statement, theme, and linked evidence items.

## 5. Live Evidence Support Profile & Triangulation Diagnostics

When authoring or editing a finding (`FindingAuthoringModal`), the workbench provides **real-time diagnostic feedback** using `computeSupportProfile(finding, scope, evidence, sources)`.

### 5.1 Support Tiers
The triangulation engine assigns an objective confidence tier based on the selected evidence base:
- `Strongly Supported`: $\ge 3$ independent sources across $\ge 2$ distinct collection methods, covering $\ge 2$ sites or explicit stakeholder subgroups, with no unresolved contradictions.
- `Moderately Supported`: Multiple independent sources with partial triangulation or single-method convergence.
- `Emerging / Needs Triangulation`: Minimal source backing (1–2 sources), single-site observation, or unverified claims.
- `Contradicted / Challenged`: Active counter-evidence or contradictions identified in the evidence base without explanatory reconciliation.

### 5.2 Mandatory Limitation Notes
To ensure methodological defensibility:
- Any finding classified in the **Emerging** tier or exhibiting documented coverage gaps strictly requires a **Limitation Note** (`limitationNote`) before it can be submitted for review or validated.
- The UI enforces this constraint in both `FindingAuthoringModal` and `handleFindingValidate`.

## 6. Downstream Outputs & Dependency Invalidation

### 6.1 Anchored Downstream Recommendations
Recommendations (`REC-***`) are anchored directly in parent findings (`findingId`). Evaluators specify:
- Action Statement & Expected Benefit
- Primary Action Owner / Responsible Institution
- Timeframe (*Immediate*, *Short-term*, *Medium-term*, *Long-term*)
- Feasibility (*High*, *Medium*, *Low*) & Implementation Risk
- Measurable Success Indicator

### 6.2 Substantive Edit Invalidation Life-Cycle
If an evaluator edits a finding that has already been `Validated`, the system protects evidentiary integrity through pure lifecycle validation functions (`src/lib/validation/validationLifecycle.ts`):

1. **Substantive Change Detection (`isSubstantiveFindingChange`)**:
   - Changes to `statement`, `explanation`, `implication`, or `evidenceIds` are classified as **substantive**.
   - Minor edits (e.g. fixing whitespace, formatting) do not trigger invalidation.
2. **Finding Invalidation (`applySubstantiveFindingEdit`)**:
   - The finding's status reverts to `Needs Review`.
   - The finding revision increments (`revision += 1`).
   - `validatedAt` and `validatedBy` are cleared.
   - An entry is appended to `invalidationHistory`.
3. **Downstream Recommendation Warning & Export Protection**:
   - Recommendations linked to the unvalidated finding display an amber warning banner:
     > `⚠️ Linked Finding requires re-validation (temporarily excluded from formal export)`
   - `isRecommendationExportEligible` evaluates to `false`.
   - The recommendation is **automatically excluded** from the formal Programme Learning Brief export.
4. **Re-validation Restoration**:
   - Once the evaluator reviews the revised finding and completes human validation (`handleFindingValidate`), `isRecommendationExportEligible` immediately returns `true`, and the recommendation is automatically restored to the export deliverable.

## 7. Traceability & Data Schema

### 7.1 Zero-Migration IndexedDB Design
To prevent brittle database migrations, all Phase 6 additions are backwards-compatible:
- `studyQuestions` array stored within `StudyMeta`.
- `patternNotes` array stored within `StudyMeta`.
- `outputConfig` object stored within `StudyMeta`.
- `studyQuestionIds` array added to `EvidenceEntry`.
- `studyQuestionId` and `limitationNote` added to `Finding`.

### 7.2 Traceability Drawer Integration
The global Traceability Drawer (`src/components/TraceabilityDrawer.tsx`) provides specialized cards for Phase 6 entities:
- **`RQ-***` (Study Question)**: Displays question text, criterion badge, assigned validated evidence chips, and associated findings.
- **`PAT-***` (Working Pattern)**: Displays pattern statement, thematic classification, contradictions, and supporting evidence base.

### 7.3 Demo Study Protection
The built-in demo case (`Community Bridges`) remains strictly read-only and immutable. Adding or editing study questions, patterns, or findings on the demo case automatically clones the case to a local editable study.
